Fit with my PhD goals

Both projects match my research interests in vascular biology and metabolic regulation. Below is how I would apply the same approach; these are proposals and training goals, not results I already have.

First choice · VTE outcomes (prospective cohort & biobank)

Why do patients differ after DVT or pulmonary embolism?

  • Rapid literature map: use the pipeline to map published work on post-VTE outcomes (recurrence, bleeding on anticoagulation, post-thrombotic syndrome, persistent dyspnoea and functional limitation after PE, psychological burden and quality of life), together with candidate biomarkers such as D-dimer.
  • Prioritise predictors: grade each patient-, event- and treatment-related factor by study design (prospective cohort, trial, registry, case–control) and how consistently it is reported. The result is an evidence-ranked shortlist of candidate predictors.
  • Focus the thesis question: use that map, together with what I see in the outpatient clinic, to select a question that the cohort data and biobank can actually answer.
  • Clinical role: as a physician I want to recruit and follow patients myself and take part in structured assessments and outcome adjudication.
  • Language: C1-certified German, which is the working language of my current degree and of my professional work in Austria. This is sufficient for informed consent and patient interviews.
  • Currently building: building on Python (NumPy, pandas) and basic SQL from JKU coursework, I am learning R and taking a statistics course at JKU this semester (exam results due February 2027).
  • PhD training goals: clinical epidemiology, longitudinal and time-to-event analysis, advanced biostatistics in R, prediction modelling with external validation, and biobank-based biomarker research.

Brain–liver axis

Central and autonomic control of liver metabolism

  • My map already covers the relevant categories: brain energy and glucose metabolism (489 papers), mitochondrial function (792), gut microbiome (650) and the gut–brain axis domain (700). It has no dedicated hepatic or autonomic-nerve entity yet.
  • The same pipeline could map human versus animal evidence on central (e.g. melanocortin/MC4R) and autonomic regulation of hepatic lipid metabolism, keeping experimental-model findings separate from human data.
  • Training goals: human metabolic intervention studies, GCP and trial conduct, in vivo metabolic phenotyping, and metabolic data analysis.

Category counts come from the neuropsychiatric corpus described above. No VTE or hepatology corpus has been built yet.
